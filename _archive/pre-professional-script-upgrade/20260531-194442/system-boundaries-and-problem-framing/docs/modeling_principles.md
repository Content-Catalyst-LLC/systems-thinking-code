# Modeling Principles

## 1. Make boundaries explicit

Every model draws a boundary. Document what is inside the analysis, what is treated as context, and what is excluded.

## 2. Compare frames

A boundary-sensitive model should support alternative frames, such as technical, institutional, ecological, justice, and governance frames.

## 3. Include externalities

Externalized costs and benefits should be documented wherever possible, even when they are difficult to quantify.

## 4. Treat stakeholder inclusion as evidence

Affected stakeholders can reveal burdens, risks, exclusions, and workarounds that formal datasets miss.

## 5. Vary time horizon

Short-term and long-term assessments may produce different conclusions.

## 6. Avoid false neutrality

Problem frames are not neutral. They reflect assumptions, values, authority, and available evidence.

## 7. Use models as aids to judgment

Boundary models should clarify assumptions and consequences. They should not replace ethical or participatory judgment.
