# Modeling Principles

## 1. Treat the part-in-relation as the unit of analysis

A component is not understood only by its internal properties. Its function and consequence depend on its relationships, dependencies, role, and context.

## 2. Distinguish component performance from system performance

A part can improve its own metric while harming the whole. Local optimization must be checked against whole-system outcomes.

## 3. Make dependencies explicit

Hidden dependencies create hidden fragility. Document what each part depends on and what depends on each part.

## 4. Examine levels of analysis

A part at one level may be a whole at another. Move up and down scales to avoid incomplete explanation.

## 5. Track failure propagation

In interdependent systems, stress can travel. Model how failure, delay, scarcity, or mistrust moves through relationships.

## 6. Connect resilience to distribution

Resilience is not merely persistence. Ask who or what absorbs stress, who has voice, and whether the system protects or consumes its parts.
