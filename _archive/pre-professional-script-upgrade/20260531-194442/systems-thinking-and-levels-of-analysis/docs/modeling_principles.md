# Modeling Principles

## 1. Match the model to the level of analysis

Do not use individual-level data to make system-level claims without justification, and do not use system-level summaries to erase local variation.

## 2. Represent nested systems explicitly

Identify which entities are nested inside other entities: people within teams, teams within organizations, neighborhoods within cities, assets within networks, and ecosystems within regions.

## 3. Separate within-level and cross-level relationships

Relationships within a level may behave differently from relationships across levels.

## 4. Watch for aggregation effects

Averages can hide distribution, clustering, inequality, outliers, and subgroup vulnerability.

## 5. Match interventions to causal levels

A micro-level support program cannot by itself solve a macro-level structural problem; a macro-level policy can fail without meso-level implementation capacity.

## 6. Treat model outputs as prompts for inquiry

Level-aware models should clarify assumptions and guide questions, not replace evidence, stakeholder knowledge, or ethical judgment.
