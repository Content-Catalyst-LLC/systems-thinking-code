# Modeling Principles

## 1. Distinguish triggers from structures

A trigger may explain timing. Structure explains vulnerability, recurrence, amplification, and unequal exposure.

## 2. Specify the causal role

Clarify whether a variable is a trigger, enabling condition, amplifier, buffer, structural cause, or historical cause.

## 3. Make feedback explicit

If an effect returns to influence its own cause, represent that loop clearly.

## 4. Represent delay and accumulation

Causal consequences often appear after a lag because stocks accumulate or deplete over time.

## 5. Test counterfactuals carefully

Intervention scenarios should ask whether the system structure changes, not only whether one event is suppressed.

## 6. Document boundaries

Every causal model depends on what variables, stakeholders, histories, and effects are included.

## 7. Treat causal modeling as inquiry

Models clarify assumptions. They do not replace domain knowledge, lived experience, empirical evidence, or ethical judgment.
