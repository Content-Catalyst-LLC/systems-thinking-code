# Modeling Principles

## 1. Begin with behavior over time

Feedback-loop analysis should explain a pattern: growth, decline, stabilization, oscillation, overshoot, collapse, or recovery.

## 2. Use variables that can rise or fall

Use names such as `public_trust`, `response_delay`, `maintenance_backlog`, `system_capacity`, and `service_demand`.

## 3. Distinguish positive and negative causal links

Positive means same-direction influence. Negative means opposite-direction influence. These are causal polarities, not moral judgments.

## 4. Separate reinforcing and balancing loops

Reinforcing loops amplify change. Balancing loops counteract change. Either can be beneficial or harmful depending on what the loop produces.

## 5. Represent delays explicitly

Delay can create overshoot, oscillation, overcorrection, and policy failure.

## 6. Document assumptions

Every feedback model depends on boundary choices, variable definitions, parameter values, time scales, and omitted relationships.

## 7. Connect modeling to ethics

Ask who benefits, who absorbs costs, whose feedback is heard, and whose signals are ignored.
