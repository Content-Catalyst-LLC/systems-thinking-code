# Modeling Principles

This article scaffold models shifting the burden as a systems archetype involving two competing pathways:

1. A symptomatic solution that reduces visible pressure quickly.
2. A fundamental solution that builds durable capacity more slowly.

The central modeling question is not whether relief works in the short term. It is whether reliance on relief decreases as fundamental capacity improves.

## Core assumptions

- Problem pressure can be reduced by symptomatic relief.
- Problem pressure can also be reduced through fundamental capacity.
- Repeated symptomatic relief may weaken capacity or reduce investment in repair.
- Externalized burden may rise when internal institutional metrics exclude public, worker, ecological, household, or future costs.
- A healthy transition reduces dependency on symptomatic solutions over time.

## Responsible interpretation

These models are conceptual demonstrations with synthetic data. They are not calibrated operational models. They should support thinking, teaching, and reproducible examples, not automated policy decisions.
