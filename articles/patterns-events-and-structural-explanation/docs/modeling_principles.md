# Modeling Principles

## 1. Separate events, patterns, and structures

An event records what happened. A pattern shows recurrence. A structure explains why recurrence is likely.

## 2. Use time as evidence

Patterns require behavior over time. A single event may be urgent, but recurrence is what signals system behavior.

## 3. Make causal pathways explicit

Structural explanation should identify relationships, incentives, rules, constraints, feedback loops, delays, resources, authority, and mental models.

## 4. Treat boundaries as choices

A narrow boundary may hide causes, displaced harms, or excluded stakeholders.

## 5. Do not confuse diagrams with proof

Maps and models clarify assumptions. They do not replace empirical validation, stakeholder knowledge, or ethical judgment.

## 6. Connect structure to responsibility

Structural explanation should not dissolve accountability. It should clarify who designed, maintained, benefited from, ignored, or failed to change harmful arrangements.
