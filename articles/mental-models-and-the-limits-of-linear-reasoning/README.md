# Mental Models and the Limits of Linear Reasoning

This article scaffold supports the Systems Thinking article **“Mental Models and the Limits of Linear Reasoning.”** It provides synthetic data, reproducible examples, and multi-language starting points for studying mental models, linear versus systemic explanations, feedback, delay, boundary critique, assumption testing, and intervention framing.

## Purpose

The repository is designed for systems-thinking education, public-interest research workflows, organizational learning, policy design, and reproducible modeling demonstrations. It uses synthetic data only and focuses on how reasoning frameworks shape what people notice, explain, and redesign.

## Structure

- `python/` — linear-versus-systems models, feedback-delay simulation, mental-model comparison, assumption sensitivity, boundary critique, and intervention-frame analysis.
- `r/` — base R summaries and output tables for comparing causal frames and assumptions.
- `julia/` — nonlinear reasoning and feedback dynamics examples.
- `sql/` — schemas for mental models, assumptions, boundaries, causal claims, feedback loops, intervention frames, model runs, and outputs.
- `rust/`, `go`, `cpp`, `fortran`, `c` — lightweight systems-language scaffolds for diagnostics and simulation.
- `docs/` — modeling principles, article notes, mental-model framework, boundary critique, ethics notes, assumptions, and responsible-use notes.
- `data/` — synthetic datasets.
- `outputs/` — generated tables and figures.
- `notebooks/` — notebook placeholders.

## Article thesis

Mental models shape what people notice and how they intervene. Linear reasoning can be useful in simple contexts, but complex systems require feedback-aware reasoning that includes delay, adaptation, accumulation, boundary critique, and power.

## Suggested workflows

```bash
python3 python/linear_vs_systemic_model.py

python3 python/mental_model_comparison.py
```
