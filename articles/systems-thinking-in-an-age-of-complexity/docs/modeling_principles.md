# Modeling Principles

This scaffold treats systems thinking in complex conditions as a practical discipline for understanding feedback, delay, resilience, learning, accountability, harm, and transformation.

## Principles

1. Model complexity pressure through interdependence, feedback, delay, adaptation, and uncertainty.
2. Treat resilience as capacity, not moral legitimacy.
3. Include boundary inclusion and affected voice in systems readiness.
4. Track harm accumulation and repair capacity.
5. Distinguish optimization from transformation.
6. Test leverage through goals, rules, information flows, and power.
7. Use models to support learning, not as final authority.
8. Pair computation with qualitative, historical, ecological, and affected-community knowledge.
